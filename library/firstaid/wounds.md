# Wounds and infection

## Cleaning a wound
1. Stop the bleeding first ([Bleeding](page:firstaid/bleeding)).
2. Wash your hands. Irrigate the wound with plenty of **clean drinkable water** (boiled and cooled is ideal). A clean plastic bag with a pin hole gives a good pressure jet. Use at least 1 litre for a dirty wound.
3. Remove visible dirt and splinters with clean tweezers (boil or flame them).
4. Don't put alcohol or hydrogen peroxide deep in the wound; they damage tissue. Iodine (povidone, diluted to weak tea colour) or soap and water around the wound is fine.
5. Cover with a clean dressing. Change daily or when wet/dirty.

## Close or leave open?
- Clean cuts less than ~6–8 h old with straight edges can be closed with wound-closure strips (Steri-Strips or tape cut into butterflies).
- **Leave open** and just cover: bites (animal and human), puncture wounds, dirty wounds, wounds older than 12 h. Closing these traps infection.

## Signs of infection
Increasing redness spreading out, swelling, heat, throbbing pain, pus, red streaks up the limb, fever. Treat: open and clean the wound, warm soaks 3× daily, rest and raise the limb. Red streaks or fever = needs antibiotics and medical help urgently.

## Tetanus
Any dirty wound, puncture or bite is a tetanus risk if the last vaccination was more than 10 years ago. Note it and get a booster as soon as care is available.

## Blisters
Small and intact: cover with a padded plaster/tape. Large and painful: clean, pierce at the edge with a sterile needle, leave the skin on, cover.

## Splinters and fish hooks
Splinter: pull out in the direction it went in. Fish hook in the skin: if the point is near the surface, **push it on through** until the barb comes out, cut the barb off with pliers, and pull the hook back out the way it came. Clean as above and watch for infection. Hooks near the eye or deep in a joint: leave in, tape it still and get help.

## Bites and stings (Europe)
- **Ticks**: remove with tick tweezers or fine tweezers, grip close to the skin, pull straight out slowly. Mark the date. A spreading red ring (≥5 cm) days to weeks later, or flu-like illness, may be Lyme disease: needs antibiotics.
- **Adder (Vipera berus)**: keep calm and still, remove rings, immobilise the limb at heart level, no cutting/sucking/tourniquet, get to hospital. Rarely fatal for adults.
- **Wasp/bee**: scrape out a bee sting, cool. Watch for [anaphylaxis](page:firstaid/shock).
- **Dog/cat/bat bite**: wash with soap and water for 15 minutes, leave open. Bats and animals from abroad: rabies risk, needs vaccination quickly.
