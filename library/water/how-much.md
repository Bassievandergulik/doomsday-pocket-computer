# How much water you need, and dehydration

## Daily need
| Situation | Litres per person per day |
|---|---|
| Resting, cool weather | 2 |
| Normal activity | 3 |
| Hard work or heat (25 °C+) | 4–6 |
| Plus cooking and basic hygiene | +1–2 |

Emergency planning guideline (EU civil protection, Red Cross): **at least 3 litres per person per day, for at least 3 days** (better: 1–2 weeks). Pets too.

## Signs of dehydration
- Dark yellow urine, peeing less than 4× a day: drink more.
- Headache, dizziness, dry mouth, tiredness: moderate.
- Confusion, no urine, fast pulse, sunken eyes, in babies a sunken soft spot and no wet nappy for 6 h: **severe**, urgent.

## Oral rehydration solution (ORS), homemade
For diarrhoea, vomiting, heat exhaustion.
- **1 litre** safe water
- **6 level teaspoons sugar** (about 30 g)
- **½ level teaspoon salt** (about 2.5–3 g)
Stir until dissolved. Should taste no saltier than tears. Too much salt is harmful: measure carefully. Adults sip 2–3 litres/day plus normal drinks; children about 100–200 ml after each loose stool. A banana or orange juice adds potassium. Make a fresh batch every day.

## Diarrhoea
Keep drinking ORS, keep eating simple foods (rice, bread, bananas, soup). Seek help for blood in stool, high fever, no urine, or more than 3 days. Wash hands, and keep the sick person's toilet separate ([Hygiene](page:skills/hygiene)).
