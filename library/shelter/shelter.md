# Shelter and staying warm

## Choose the spot
- Flat, dry, slightly raised ground. Not in hollows (cold air and water collect), not on river banks or dry stream beds (flash floods), not under dead trees or big dead branches ("widowmakers").
- Out of the wind: lee side of a hill, wood or wall. Check the weather tab: falling pressure means wind and rain are coming.
- Near water and firewood but at least 50 m from water (insects, damp, animals).
- Use **Find → Shelter** for mapped huts and shelters nearby.

## Insulate from the ground first
The ground drains heat far faster than air. Make a bed of **20–30 cm** of dry leaves, grass, bracken, spruce branches, a foam mat or backpack. Compressed it should still be a hand thick.

## Quick shelters
- **Tarp A-frame**: line between two trees at chest height, tarp over, edges pegged down. Low end into the wind.
- **Tarp lean-to**: one edge high, one pegged to the ground facing away from the wind; fire in front with a reflector wall behind it.
- **Debris hut** (no tarp): a ridge pole from a stump or fork at hip height to the ground, ribs leaning on both sides, then **at least 60–90 cm** of leaves piled on top. Make it just big enough for your body, fill the inside with dry leaves too. Warm enough for cold nights without a sleeping bag if thick enough.
- **Snow**: a snow trench or quinzhee (heap, let settle 1–2 h, hollow out, walls 30 cm, vent hole). Snow insulates; never sleep directly on it.
- In buildings: pick one small room, ideally interior and south-facing, close it off, hang blankets over windows and doors, everyone sleeps together.

## Clothing: COLD
**C**lean, avoid **O**verheating (vent before you sweat), wear **L**oose **L**ayers, keep **D**ry. Wool and synthetics keep warmth when wet; **cotton kills** in the cold. Hat and dry socks for sleeping. Put tomorrow's clothes inside your sleeping bag.

## Sleeping warm
Eat something fatty before bed, pee before sleeping, a bottle of hot water (wrapped) in the bag, sleep in pairs.

## Carbon monoxide
Never use a stove, barbecue, generator or charcoal inside a tent, car or closed room. Headache, dizziness and sleepiness that improves outdoors = get to fresh air immediately.
