# Making water safe to drink

> Assume all surface water (rivers, ponds, canals, rain barrels) contains germs. In farmland and towns it may also contain chemicals that **boiling and filters do not remove**.

## Methods compared
| Method | Kills/removes germs | Removes chemicals | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boiling** | Yes, all (bacteria, viruses, parasites) | No | Rolling boil **1 minute**; 3 minutes above 2000 m. Most reliable. |
| Ceramic/hollow-fibre filter (0.1–0.2 µm, e.g. Sawyer, Katadyn) | Bacteria & protozoa, **not** reliably viruses | No (some carbon ones a little) | Good in Europe's wilderness; add chlorine downstream of towns/farms |
| Chlorine tablets (NaDCC) or drops | Bacteria & viruses, not Cryptosporidium | No | Follow pack; usually 30 min wait, longer if cold |
| Chlorine dioxide tablets | Bacteria, viruses, protozoa incl. Crypto | No | Wait time up to 4 h for Crypto |
| Household bleach (unscented, 4–6% chlorine) | Bacteria & viruses | No | **2 drops per litre** clear water, 4 drops if cloudy/cold. Shake, wait 30 min. Should smell faintly of chlorine; if not, repeat and wait 15 min more. |
| SODIS (sun) | Most germs | No | Clear PET bottle ≤2 L, clear water, lay in full sun **6 hours** (2 days if cloudy) |
| Activated carbon | Some | Many chemicals, taste, smell | Use after the germ treatment |
| Distillation | Yes | Yes, including salt | Slow, needs fuel. The only way to make **seawater** drinkable. |

## Step by step
1. **Choose the best source**: running water upstream of farms and roads, springs, rain. Avoid water near dead animals, mines, industry, scum or oily film.
2. **Clear it**: let it settle for an hour, then pour through a cloth, coffee filter or sand/charcoal layers. Cloudy water makes chemical treatment fail.
3. **Disinfect**: boil, filter, or chemical as above.
4. **Store** in clean covered containers, use a ladle or tap so hands don't go in. Re-treat stored water after a few days.

## Improvised filter (pre-filter only, does not make water safe on its own)
Bottle with the bottom cut off, upside down: cloth in the neck, then crushed charcoal (from your fire, not ash), fine sand, coarse sand, gravel on top. Pour slowly.

## Never drink
- Seawater or urine (makes dehydration worse).
- Water from car radiators, swimming pools long untreated, or flooded areas near chemicals.
- Snow or ice unmelted (cools you down): melt it first, then treat.

## Hidden water at home
Hot water boiler/tank (often 80–200 L; turn off power/gas first, drain from the bottom tap), toilet cistern (not the bowl, and not if it contains blue cleaner), ice cubes, canned vegetables, the pipes (open the lowest tap).
