# Cold, snow and ice

Cold kills by wind and wetness more than by low temperature. The rule: **stay dry, out of the wind, and insulated from the ground.** Treat hypothermia early: [Hypothermia and frostbite](page:firstaid/hypothermia).

## Clothing
Layers you can open and remove: a thin base layer (wool or synthetic, **no cotton**), an insulating middle layer, a wind- and waterproof shell. Take layers **off before you sweat** when working, put them on as soon as you stop. Dry wet socks and gloves against your body in the sleeping bag. Cover head, neck and wrists.

## Shelter in snow
- **Snow trench**: dig a trench as long as you are and about 1 m deep, roof it with branches, a tarp or snow blocks. Quick and warm.
- **Snow cave**: dig into a deep drift, the sleeping platform higher than the entrance (warm air rises, cold flows out). Make a **ventilation hole** with a stick in the roof and keep it open: carbon monoxide and CO2 build up from candles and stoves.

![Snow cave: sleep higher than the entrance](fig:snow-cave.svg)

- **Quinzhee**: pile snow 2 m high over your bags, wait 1–2 hours to set, dig it hollow, walls about 30 cm (push sticks in from outside as depth guides).
- Insulate underneath with 10 cm of branches, a pack, a mat. Close the door with a pack or snow block.
- Inside a snow shelter it stays around 0 °C even at −20 °C outside.

## Water
**Melt snow, don't eat it**: eating it cools your core. Put a little water in the pot first, then add snow little by little (dry snow alone scorches the pot). Ice gives more water than snow for the same fuel. Purify meltwater from places animals and people use.

## Ice
- Safe to walk only on clear, solid ice at least **10 cm** thick; snow-covered, grey or porous ice, ice near reeds, bridges and inflows is weak.
- Spread your weight, carry a long pole crosswise, test ahead.
- If you fall in: don't panic, the first minute is cold shock; turn back the way you came (that ice held), put arms on the ice, kick your legs horizontal and slide out on your belly. **Roll** away; don't stand up. Get dry and warm at once.

![Getting out through ice](fig:ice-rescue.svg)

- Rescuing someone: lie flat, reach with a branch, rope or scarf. Never go to them on foot.

## Snow and mountain dangers
- **Avalanches**: most happen on slopes of 30–45°, in the day after heavy snow, wind or quick warming. Travel on ridges or flat valley floors away from the bottom of steep slopes; one at a time across a risky slope.
- **Snow blindness**: painful, gritty eyes hours after sun on snow. Prevent with sunglasses or a strip of bark or cloth with narrow slits. Treat by covering the eyes and resting in the dark for a day or two.
- **Whiteout**: stop and shelter; you lose direction within metres. The Go tab's compass and back-track keep pointing even when you can't see.

## Fire and food
Build the fire on a platform of green logs so it doesn't sink into the snow. Eat fat and carbohydrates often: your body burns more in the cold. A warm drink and snack before sleeping keeps you warmer through the night.
