# Survival at sea

> **Mayday**: VHF channel 16 (or 2182 kHz): "Mayday, Mayday, Mayday, this is [boat name] ×3, Mayday [name], position [read it from the Map/Go tab], nature of distress, number of people." Activate an EPIRB/PLB if you have one: it is the single most effective thing you can do.

## Abandoning a boat
- **Step up into the life raft**, don't step down: stay with the boat until it is really sinking. A boat is easier to find and safer than a raft.
- Before you go: grab the grab bag, this device, **water**, warm clothes, a hat, the VHF/EPIRB, flares, a knife and a bucket.
- Wear a lifejacket. In the water: **HELP position** (knees to chest, arms crossed) alone, **huddle** in a group. Don't swim to stay warm: it loses heat faster.

![HELP position and huddle](fig:help-huddle.svg)

- Cold water: the first minute brings gasping (cold shock). Keep your head out and breathe slowly before doing anything. You have about 10 minutes of useful movement, then about an hour before hypothermia makes you unconscious (less below 10 °C).

## In the raft: first actions
1. Cut the line to the sinking boat, get clear, stream the **sea anchor** (drogue) to slow drift and stay near the last position.
2. Pull others in, close the canopy, bail out water, dry yourself and others.
3. Take seasickness tablets at once (vomiting loses water). Check for injuries and leaks.
4. Organise watches: 1–2 hours each, looking for ships, planes, land, rain.

## Water: the real limit
- **Never drink seawater**, and never urine. It speeds up dehydration and causes madness and death.
- **No water for the first 24 hours** if you are healthy: your body saves water. After that about 0.5 litre per day if you have it.
- Catch **rain** with the canopy, a tarp, clothing. Rinse the salt off first with the first rain. Store in every container.
- Don't eat if you have no water: digestion (especially protein: fish, meat) uses water. Carbohydrates (sweets, biscuits) are fine.
- Reduce sweating: shade, wet clothes with seawater in heat (not your skin if sore), rest by day.
- Solar stills and hand-pump desalinators (if packed) work; check them every day.

## Food
Fish gather in the raft's shade. Line, hook, bait from fish guts; a knife lashed to an oar as a spear. Eat fish fresh, only with enough water. Avoid fish with spines, beak-like mouths, puffed bodies, or that smell bad. Birds that land on the raft can be caught by hand. Seaweed: only small amounts, salty.

## Health
- Salt-water sores: keep dry, don't squeeze. Move toes and legs to avoid immersion foot.
- Sunburn and eye damage: cover skin, improvise sunglasses from cloth with slits.
- Hypothermia: dry clothes, huddle, cover heads. See [Hypothermia](page:firstaid/hypothermia).

## Being found
- Stay with the raft. Use flares **only** when you see or hear a ship or aircraft (hand flare by day orange smoke, at night red). A mirror flash is visible from far: see [Signalling](page:comms/signals).
- Radar reflector up, bright colours out, torch/screen SOS at night. Signal flags: [Ship signal flags](page:signs/signal-flags).

## Signs of land
Clouds that stay put over the horizon (often above islands), a green tint on cloud bottoms (lagoons), birds flying in a straight line at dusk (towards land) or dawn (away), floating plants and wood, insects, a change in swell pattern, murky water. Land at a sandy, sheltered spot; avoid surf on rocks and coral. Keep shoes and clothes on when landing.
