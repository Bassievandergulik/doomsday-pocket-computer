# Heat, desert and tropical islands

Hot places kill through **water loss** and **heat stroke** long before hunger. Islands like Curaçao, Aruba and Bonaire are dry, windy and sunny, with cactus scrub, little fresh water and strong sun all year.

## Water first
- You need **4–6 litres a day** in the heat when active, more if working. Ration sweat, not water: drink what you have and stay cool, instead of saving it in the bottle.
- Rest in the shade from about 11:00 to 16:00; travel and work at dawn, dusk and by night.
- Where to look: green vegetation, dry stream beds (dig at the outside of bends, the lowest point), rock hollows after rain, birds flying low at dusk. On islands: rain catchment, cisterns, wells. Always purify: [Purifying water](page:water/purifying).
- Morning dew: wipe it from plants or a tarp with a cloth and wring it out.
- **Coconuts**: green (young) nuts hold clean water; ripe brown ones have little, and too much of their milk is laxative.
- A **solar still** (hole, container in the middle, plastic sheet with a stone on top) gives only about 0.5 litre a day: not enough on its own, but useful for purifying urine or seawater.

## Heat illness
Headache, dizziness, nausea, cramps, dark urine = **heat exhaustion**: shade, lie down, drink, cool with water. Hot dry or confused person = **heat stroke**, an emergency: cool immediately with water and wind. See [Heat illness](page:firstaid/heat).

## Sun and clothing
Cover up: long, loose, light-coloured clothes; a hat with a neck flap; sunglasses (or a cloth with slits). Keep clothes on: they keep sweat on your skin longer. Sit on something; the ground can be 20 °C hotter than the air. The UV index on the Weather tab tells you how fast skin burns.

## Shelter
Shade with airflow: a tarp or cloth raised 30–50 cm above you, double layer with a gap if possible. Don't sleep in dry stream beds (flash floods from far-away rain). Shake out shoes and clothes in the morning (scorpions, spiders).

## Dangerous plants and animals (Caribbean)
- **Manchineel** (Dutch: *manzanilla*, Papiamentu: *manzaniya*): a beach tree with shiny leaves and small green "apples". Everything is poisonous: one bite of the fruit causes severe throat swelling; sap and even rainwater dripping from it blister skin and can blind. Never shelter under it or burn its wood (toxic smoke). Often marked with red paint or signs.
- **Cactus spines** and **thorny acacia**: wear shoes; pull spines out with tape.
- Sea: **sea urchins** (black spines: don't dig them out, soak in hot water), **fire coral** (burning rash: rinse with seawater and vinegar, not fresh water), **lionfish** (venomous spines: hot water as hot as can be tolerated for 30–90 min), **jellyfish** (rinse with seawater, vinegar for box jellyfish). See [Dangerous animals](page:wild/dangerous-animals).
- **Mosquitoes** carry dengue, zika and chikungunya: cover skin at dusk and dawn, use a net and repellent, empty standing water.

## Food
Fish and shellfish near shore (avoid big reef predators like barracuda: ciguatera poisoning), sea grapes (*druif di laman*, edible), prickly pear fruit (remove the fine spines by burning or rubbing in sand), cactus pads (young, spines removed, cooked). Eat less in heat if water is short.

## Desert travel
Follow the coast or dry valleys, mark your route, keep the sun on a known side. Mirages are common: water seen "on the road" is reflected sky. Sandstorm: cover face, sit with your back to the wind, mark your direction before it hits.
