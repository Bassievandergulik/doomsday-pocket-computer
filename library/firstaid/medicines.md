# Basic medicine kit and doses

> Always read the leaflet in the pack. Doses below are **standard adult** doses for common over-the-counter medicines. Children need weight-based doses from the pack. Check allergies, pregnancy and other medicines.

| Medicine | Use | Usual adult dose | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paracetamol 500 mg | Pain, fever | 1–2 tablets every 4–6 h, **max 8 tablets (4 g) in 24 h** | Liver damage in overdose; many combination products also contain it. Max 2 g/day with heavy alcohol use or low body weight. |
| Ibuprofen 200/400 mg | Pain, fever, inflammation, sprains | 200–400 mg every 6–8 h with food, max 1200 mg/day without a doctor | Not with stomach ulcers, kidney disease, dehydration, late pregnancy; can worsen asthma |
| Cetirizine/loratadine 10 mg | Hay fever, hives, itchy bites | 1 tablet daily | Does not treat anaphylaxis |
| Loperamide 2 mg | Diarrhoea | 2 caps then 1 after each loose stool, max 8/day (6 in some packs) | Not with fever or blood in stool. Rehydration matters more. |
| ORS sachets | Rehydration | 1 sachet per litre of safe water | See [Water needs](page:water/how-much) for a homemade recipe |
| Povidone iodine | Wound cleaning | Dilute | Not with iodine allergy or thyroid disease |
| Potassium iodide (KI) | Only on government instruction after a nuclear accident | As instructed | Protects the thyroid only; useless otherwise. Over-40s often don't need it. |

## Kit list
Gloves, trauma dressings, gauze, crêpe bandage, triangular bandage, tape, wound-closure strips, plasters, blister plasters, tick tweezers, scissors, tweezers, safety pins, foil blanket, tourniquet, burn gel/cling film, thermometer, the medicines above, personal prescriptions (keep a 1–3 month stock if possible and rotate it).

## Medicine storage
Cool, dry, dark. Most tablets stay effective for a while past their date; liquids, insulin and adrenaline pens do not keep as well. Insulin can be kept up to ~4 weeks at room temperature once open; a clay-pot evaporation cooler helps in summer.
