# Finding water

Use **Find → Water** first: it lists mapped drinking taps, wells and springs with distance and direction. Taps in cemeteries are common in NL/BE/DE (often marked "no drinking water" though: treat it).

## Where water collects
- **Low ground**: valleys, base of cliffs, outside bends of dry stream beds (dig 30–60 cm and wait).
- **Green vegetation** in an otherwise dry area: willows, reeds, rushes, alder mean water near the surface.
- **Animal paths** heading downhill often lead to water, especially at dawn and dusk. Swarms of insects and birds flying low and straight at dawn/dusk too.
- **Springs** at the foot of hills where rock layers change.
- In towns: rain gutters and downpipes, ponds in parks, fountains, swimming pools (good for washing; treat before drinking).

## Collecting
- **Rain**: tarp or poncho tied with a low centre over a container. Roof runoff: let the first few minutes wash the roof, then collect. Treat before drinking.
- **Dew**: tie cloth around your ankles and walk through wet grass at dawn, wring it out. Can give 0.5–1 L in an hour in the right conditions.
- **Transpiration bag**: clear plastic bag tied over a leafy (non-poisonous) branch in sun, small stone in the low corner. A few hundred ml per day.
- **Solar still**: works poorly, uses more sweat to dig than it makes. Last resort.

## Rationing
Don't ration water you have when you are thirsty and working: **drink it, and ration sweat instead**. Work in the cool hours, stay in shade, don't smoke, eat less if water is very short (digestion uses water). See [How much water](page:water/how-much).
