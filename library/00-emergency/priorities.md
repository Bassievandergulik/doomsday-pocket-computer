# Survival priorities: what to do first

> If phones work at all, call **112** (all of Europe, free, works on any network and without a SIM in most countries). Say WHERE first, then WHAT happened.

## The rule of threes (rough limits)
| Without | You can survive about |
|---|---|
| Air, or with severe bleeding | 3 minutes |
| Shelter in harsh cold or heat | 3 hours |
| Water | 3 days |
| Food | 3 weeks |

So the order is usually: **safety → first aid → shelter/warmth → water → signalling → food.**

## STOP when things go wrong
1. **S**it down. Panic kills more people than hunger.
2. **T**hink. What is the most urgent danger in the next hour?
3. **O**bserve. Weather, daylight left (see the Go tab for sunset), water, people, tools.
4. **P**lan. Then act, one thing at a time.

## First 10 minutes checklist
- Danger to you? Fire, traffic, water, falling objects, gas. Move away first.
- Anyone injured? Check breathing and major bleeding: [CPR](page:firstaid/cpr), [Bleeding](page:firstaid/bleeding).
- Get out of wind and wet. Cold and wet kills fast even at +10 °C: [Hypothermia](page:firstaid/hypothermia).
- Mark your position as a waypoint (Go tab → Mark here).
- Note how much water and food you have.

## Stay or move?
**Stay** if someone knows where you are, you have shelter, or you are injured. Searchers look along your planned route.
**Move** if your location is dangerous, there is no water, or nobody will look for you. Leave a note with direction and time.

## Daily routine in a long emergency
- Morning: check weather tab, plan water and fire wood.
- Keep a written log: date, food and water stocks, health, radio news.
- Sleep warm, dry and off the ground. Rest during the hottest part of the day in summer.
- Keep hands clean and drinking water separate from washing water: [Hygiene](page:skills/hygiene).
