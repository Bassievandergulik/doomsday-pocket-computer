# Hygiene and sanitation

Diarrhoeal disease is one of the biggest killers after disasters. Simple rules prevent most of it.

## Handwashing
With soap and water for 20 seconds: after the toilet, before cooking and eating, after touching the sick or dead animals. No running water: a **tippy tap** (container hung so it tips and pours, foot-operated with a string) or a bottle with a hole in the cap. Ash or sand rub works if there is no soap. Hand sanitiser (60%+ alcohol) when hands are not visibly dirty.

## Toilet when the water is off
- **Two-bucket system** (Dutch/UK government advice): one bucket for urine (pee is fairly sterile; dilute 1:8 and pour on garden or down a drain), one with a lid and a bag for poo. Cover each use with sawdust, cat litter, soil, ash or shredded paper. Seal and store bags away from living space.
- Or line the normal toilet bowl with two strong bin bags.
- Outdoors: dig a **cathole 15–20 cm deep**, at least **60 m from water**, camp and paths. Group: a trench latrine, cover with soil after each use.

## Water separation
Keep drinking water separate from washing water. Never dip hands or cups into the drinking container: use a ladle or tap.

## Food hygiene
Cook thoroughly, eat hot. Keep raw meat separate. Wash dishes in hot water with a few drops of bleach in the rinse.

## Waste
Bag and store rubbish away from living areas (rats, flies). Burn only paper and wood; plastic fumes are toxic.

## Body
- Wash daily with a cloth and a bowl of water: armpits, groin, feet. Change socks and underwear often.
- Teeth: brush or chew a frayed twig (birch, willow).
- Check for ticks every evening.
- Menstrual products: a menstrual cup or washable pads last for years.

## Dead bodies
They are not a disease risk unless the person died of cholera, Ebola or similar, but they distress people and contaminate water. Note identity, cover, move away from water sources, report when possible.
