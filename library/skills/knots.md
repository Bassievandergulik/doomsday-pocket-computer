# Essential knots

| Knot | Use |
|---|---|
| **Bowline** | Fixed loop that won't slip or jam. Rescue loop, tie to a tree. |
| **Clove hitch** | Quick attach to a post or pole. Start of lashings. |
| **Taut-line hitch** | Adjustable tension for tent and tarp guy lines. |
| **Trucker's hitch** | Pull a line very tight (ridge lines, loads). |
| **Sheet bend** | Join two ropes, also of different thickness. |
| **Figure-eight** | Stopper knot; figure-eight loop for climbing. |
| **Square (reef) knot** | Tie bandages and slings. Not for joining ropes under load. |
| **Prusik** | Grips another rope, slides when loose; for climbing a rope or adjustable tarp attachments. |

## How to tie
Every knot has a **standing part** (the long rope that takes the load) and an **end** (the short part you tie with). Tap a picture to see it bigger.

### Bowline
Make a small loop in the standing part ("rabbit hole"), with the end-side lying **on top**. The end comes **up** through the hole, **around** the back of the standing part ("round the tree"), and **back down** the hole. Pull tight. Leave a tail 10× rope thickness.

![Bowline in 3 steps](fig:knot-bowline.svg)

### Clove hitch
Wrap around the pole, cross over the first turn, wrap again and tuck the end under the second wrap.

![Clove hitch](fig:knot-clove-hitch.svg)

### Taut-line hitch
Around the peg and back. With the end make two turns around the standing line **inside** the loop, then one turn **outside** (further up), tuck. Slide the knot to tension.

![Taut-line hitch](fig:knot-taut-line.svg)

### Trucker's hitch
Make a slip loop in the line halfway, pass the end around the anchor and back through the loop, pull (2:1 pull), lock with two half hitches.

![Trucker's hitch](fig:knot-truckers.svg)

### Sheet bend
Bight in the thicker rope; the thin end comes up through the bight, around behind both legs, and tucks under itself (not under the bight).

![Sheet bend](fig:knot-sheet-bend.svg)

### Figure-eight
Make a loop, take the end around behind the standing part, then down through the loop. For a **figure-eight loop**, tie it with the rope doubled.

![Figure-eight stopper](fig:knot-figure-eight.svg)

### Square (reef) knot
Right over left and under, then left over right and under.

![Square knot](fig:knot-square.svg)

### Prusik
Tie a loop of thinner cord (closed with a double fisherman's knot). Put it behind the thick rope, pass the loop through itself 3 times so it wraps around, then pull the turns neat and side by side. Slide it when loose; it grips when loaded.

![Prusik](fig:knot-prusik.svg)

### Two half hitches
Around the object, end around the standing part and through, repeat in the same direction.

![Two half hitches](fig:knot-half-hitches.svg)

## Lashing (shelter frames, stretchers)
Square lashing: clove hitch on one pole, wrap over-under around both poles 3–4 times, then 2–3 "frapping" turns between the poles to tighten, finish with a clove hitch.

![Square lashing](fig:knot-square-lashing.svg)

## Improvised cordage
Inner bark of willow, lime (linden) or nettle stems, twisted reverse-wrap style. Paracord can be gutted: the 7 inner strands are thread or fishing line.
