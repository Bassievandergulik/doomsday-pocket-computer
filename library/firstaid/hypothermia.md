# Hypothermia and frostbite

Wet + wind + exhaustion can cause hypothermia even at +10 °C. Check the Weather tab: frost warnings mean real risk overnight.

## Signs
| Stage | Signs |
|---|---|
| Mild (35–32 °C) | Shivering, cold pale skin, clumsy hands, "umbles": stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles |
| Moderate (32–28 °C) | Shivering **stops**, confused, drowsy, slow breathing, irrational (may undress) |
| Severe (<28 °C) | Unconscious, very slow or no detectable pulse/breathing |

## Treatment
1. Get out of wind and wet. Insulate from the **ground** (mats, branches, packs).
2. Replace wet clothing with dry, or put a waterproof layer over wet clothes if you cannot change.
3. Wrap in sleeping bag/blankets, cover the head. A "hypo wrap": insulation, then plastic/foil, then more insulation.
4. Warm sweet drinks **only if fully alert** and able to swallow. No alcohol.
5. Heat packs or warm water bottles (wrapped) on the **chest, armpits, neck and groin**, not hands and feet.
6. Body-to-body warmth in a sleeping bag works.
7. Handle **gently**, keep them lying flat in moderate/severe cases: rough movement can trigger cardiac arrest.
8. No signs of life: CPR. "Nobody is dead until warm and dead".

## Prevention
Layers (wicking, insulating, windproof), wool or synthetic not cotton, hat and gloves, eat and drink regularly, stop and shelter **before** exhausted.

## Frostbite
White/grey, hard, numb skin on fingers, toes, nose, ears.
- Don't rub it. Don't rewarm if there is any chance it will freeze again.
- Rewarm in water at **37–39 °C** (comfortable to your elbow) for 15–30 min; it will hurt a lot. Painkiller beforehand helps.
- Pad between fingers/toes, keep dry, don't burst blisters, get medical help.

## Trench / immersion foot
Feet cold and wet for days: swollen, numb, then painful. Dry feet, warm slowly, elevate, change socks daily; dry your feet every night.
