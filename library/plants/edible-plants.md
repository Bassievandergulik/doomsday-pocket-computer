# Edible and useful plants (Europe)

Only eat a plant when **every** feature matches, and never from roadsides, sprayed fields or polluted water. Start with a small amount. Learn these few really well instead of many vaguely. The [deadly plants](page:plants/poisonous-plants) page shows what each one can be confused with.

## Stinging nettle
![Stinging nettle](photo:stinging-nettle.jpg)

*Urtica dioica* · NL: brandnetel · ES: ortiga

- **Recognise**: upright, 0.5–1.5 m, square stem, opposite heart-shaped leaves with saw-tooth edges, covered in stinging hairs; drooping strings of tiny green flowers
- **Eat**: young top leaves, boiled or steamed 1–2 min (stops the sting): like spinach, rich in protein and iron; tea from dried leaves
- **Also**: stem fibres make strong cord ([Tools and cordage](page:wild/tools-and-cordage))
- **Confuse with**: dead-nettles (no sting, also edible): harmless

## Dandelion
![Dandelion](photo:dandelion.jpg)

*Taraxacum officinale* · NL: paardenbloem · ES: diente de león

- **Recognise**: flat rosette of deeply toothed leaves, single yellow flower per hollow stem, white milky sap, round seed clock
- **Eat**: all of it: young leaves raw (older ones bitter: boil), flowers raw or fried, roots roasted as coffee
- **Confuse with**: other yellow 'dandelions' with branched or hairy stems: mostly also edible but bitter

## Broadleaf and ribwort plantain
![Broadleaf and ribwort plantain](photo:plantain.jpg)

*Plantago major / P. lanceolata* · NL: grote weegbree / smalle weegbree · ES: llantén

- **Recognise**: low rosette; leaves with 3–7 strong parallel veins that stay as strings when torn; flower spikes on leafless stalks. Grows on paths and lawns
- **Eat**: young leaves raw or cooked; seeds
- **Use**: **wound leaf**: chew or crush a leaf and put it on stings, insect bites, small cuts and blisters

## Wild garlic
![Wild garlic](photo:wild-garlic.jpg)

*Allium ursinum* · NL: daslook · ES: ajo de oso

- **Recognise**: spring, damp shady woods, carpets of bright green broad leaves each on its own stalk, starry white flowers in a ball; **strong garlic smell when crushed**
- **Eat**: leaves and flowers raw or cooked
- **Danger**: looks like deadly **lily of the valley** and **autumn crocus** (no garlic smell). Crush each leaf and smell it, and wash your hands between leaves, or the smell stays on your fingers

## Blackberry and raspberry
![Blackberry and raspberry](photo:blackberry.jpg)

*Rubus fruticosus / R. idaeus* · NL: braam / framboos · ES: zarzamora / frambuesa

- **Recognise**: thorny arching canes, leaves of 3–5 toothed leaflets, white or pink flowers; berries made of many small round parts
- **Eat**: ripe berries raw, late summer; young leaves as tea
- **Confuse with**: nothing dangerous in Europe: all berries built of little bubbles are edible

## Bilberry
![Bilberry](photo:bilberry.jpg)

*Vaccinium myrtillus* · NL: blauwe bosbes · ES: arándano

- **Recognise**: low shrub (20–60 cm) in acid woods and heath, green angular twigs, small oval leaves, single blue-black berries with a crown-shaped dimple, purple inside
- **Eat**: berries raw
- **Confuse with**: **deadly nightshade** (single shiny black berry with a green star of sepals, on a tall herb, not a shrub)

## Elder
![Elder](photo:elder.jpg)

*Sambucus nigra* · NL: gewone vlier · ES: saúco

- **Recognise**: shrub or small tree, corky bark, leaves of 5–7 toothed leaflets, flat plates of creamy white scented flowers (June), then hanging black berries
- **Eat**: flowers (fritters, tea), **ripe** berries only **cooked** (raw berries, leaves and stems make you sick)
- **Confuse with**: **hemlock** and **water-dropwort** also have white flat flower heads but are herbs with hollow stems and fern-like leaves: elder is a woody shrub

## Sweet chestnut
![Sweet chestnut](photo:sweet-chestnut.jpg)

*Castanea sativa* · NL: tamme kastanje · ES: castaño

- **Recognise**: long narrow leaves with sharp teeth; nuts in a **very spiny** green husk (like a hedgehog), 2–3 flat nuts with a pointed tip
- **Eat**: nuts roasted or boiled: very good energy food
- **Danger**: **horse chestnut** (conker) is poisonous: hand-shaped leaves of 5–7 leaflets, husk with few short spikes, one round shiny nut without a point

## Hazel, beech and oak
![Hazel, beech and oak](photo:hazel.jpg)

*Corylus avellana / Fagus sylvatica / Quercus robur* · NL: hazelaar / beuk / eik · ES: avellano / haya / roble

- **Hazelnuts**: round toothed leaves, nuts in a leafy frilled cup (Aug–Sept): eat raw
- **Beech nuts**: small triangular nuts in a bristly 4-part husk: eat a few raw or roast them (large amounts cause stomach upset)
- **Acorns**: bitter tannins must be removed: shell, chop, soak or boil in several changes of water until no longer bitter, then roast or grind into flour

## Cattail / bulrush
![Cattail / bulrush](photo:cattail.jpg)

*Typha latifolia* · NL: grote lisdodde · ES: espadaña / enea

- **Recognise**: in shallow water, tall flat sword-like leaves, brown sausage-shaped flower head on a stiff stalk
- **Eat**: peeled white base of young shoots (raw or cooked), starch from the roots (crush in water, let settle), young green flower spikes boiled
- **Also**: the fluff is great tinder and insulation. Only from clean water

## Chickweed, wood sorrel and clover
![Chickweed, wood sorrel and clover](photo:chickweed.jpg)

*Stellaria media / Oxalis acetosella / Trifolium repens* · NL: vogelmuur / witte klaverzuring / witte klaver · ES: pamplina / acederilla / trébol blanco

- **Chickweed**: low sprawling, tiny white star flowers with 5 deeply split petals, **one line of hairs** along the stem: raw like salad
- **Wood sorrel**: clover-like heart-shaped leaflets, sour lemony taste, white flowers with purple veins: small amounts raw (oxalic acid)
- **White clover**: leaves and flowers cooked or as tea

## Dog rose (rose hips)
![Dog rose (rose hips)](photo:dog-rose.jpg)

*Rosa canina* · NL: hondsroos · ES: rosal silvestre / escaramujo

- **Recognise**: thorny arching shrub, pink-white 5-petalled flowers, red oval hips in autumn
- **Eat**: the flesh of the hips, very rich in vitamin C. Remove the hairy seeds (they irritate): best as tea or syrup through a cloth

## Lime / linden tree
![Lime / linden tree](photo:lime-tree.jpg)

*Tilia cordata* · NL: linde · ES: tilo

- **Recognise**: heart-shaped leaves with uneven base, sweet-smelling flowers hanging from a pale leaf-like wing
- **Eat**: young leaves raw (mild salad), flowers as tea. Inner bark makes cord

## Pine
![Pine](photo:scots-pine.jpg)

*Pinus sylvestris* · NL: grove den · ES: pino silvestre

- **Recognise**: needles in **pairs** (or bundles of 3–5 in other pines), cones, resin smell
- **Use**: tea of fresh needles (vitamin C), inner bark edible in an emergency, resin as glue and fire starter
- **Danger**: **yew** has single flat needles in two rows and red berries, **no cones**: deadly. Don't use any needle tree you can't identify; pregnant women should avoid pine tea

## Purslane
![Purslane](photo:purslane.jpg)

*Portulaca oleracea* · NL: postelein · ES: verdolaga

- **Recognise**: low creeping, thick reddish stems, fleshy spoon-shaped leaves, tiny yellow flowers; Europe and the Caribbean
- **Eat**: raw or cooked, slightly sour
- **Confuse with**: spurges (Euphorbia): white milky sap, toxic. Purslane has clear sap

## Healing helpers
![Healing helpers](photo:yarrow.jpg)

*Achillea millefolium / Salix / Sphagnum / Betula* · NL: duizendblad / wilg / veenmos / berk · ES: milenrama / sauce / esfagno / abedul

- **Yarrow**: feathery leaves split into many tiny segments, flat white flower heads; crushed leaves help stop bleeding of small cuts
- **Willow bark**: inner bark chewed or as tea relieves pain and fever (contains salicin, like aspirin; not for children or with blood thinners)
- **Sphagnum moss**: bog moss, holds lots of water, mildly antiseptic: a clean dressing when nothing else is available (squeeze out, use the clean tops)
- **Birch**: white papery bark: superb tinder; spring sap is drinkable
