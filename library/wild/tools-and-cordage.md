# Tools, cordage and containers

Your **knife** is your most important tool, then something to carry and boil water in, then cord. Everything else can be made.

## Knife
- Fixed blade, 8–12 cm, full tang if possible. Keep it dry and lightly oiled; cut **away** from your body, sit with legs apart.
- **Sharpening**: on a whetstone, a smooth river stone with fine sand, or the unglazed ring on the bottom of a mug. Hold the blade at about 20° and push it as if cutting a thin slice off the stone; same number of strokes on both sides; finish with light strokes. Test on paper or a hair.

![Sharpening at about 20 degrees](fig:knife-sharpening.svg)

- **Batoning**: to split wood, put the blade on the end of a log and hit its spine with another piece of wood. Use only a sturdy fixed blade.

![Batoning](fig:batoning.svg)

- Lost your knife: break flint or quartz for a sharp edge (very sharp, careful), or grind a piece of metal on stone.

## Cordage
- From clothing: laces, seams, the inner strands of paracord (7 strands of 0.5 m each per 0.5 m of cord).
- From plants: **stinging nettle** (late summer stems: crush, split, peel the fibres off the woody core, dry), **lime/linden** inner bark (soak in water for weeks), willow and bramble (strip thorns), long grasses, cattail leaves.
- **Reverse wrap** to make strong cord: twist a bundle of fibres until it kinks into two strands, then twist each strand away from you and wrap it over the other towards you; repeat, adding new fibres before one runs out.

![Reverse wrap cordage](fig:reverse-wrap.svg)

- Knots: [Knots](page:skills/knots).

## Containers and cooking
- Metal is best: a mug or tin can over the fire.
- Without metal: **hot stones** dropped into water in a wooden bowl, a bark container, a hole lined with a plastic bag or skin. Use dry stones from high ground: wet river stones can explode in fire.
- Plastic bottles and bags can heat water if full and kept away from flames (only the water touches the heat).
- Birch bark folds into watertight boxes and cups; also excellent fire starter.

## Other things to improvise
- **Needle**: a sliver of bone or hard wood with a hole made by a hot wire.
- **Glue**: pine resin melted with a little charcoal powder (pitch glue); also covers small wounds.
- **Snowshoes**: bent green branches in an oval with cross-lashings.
- **Backpack**: a blanket or tarp rolled around your gear and tied in a horseshoe over one shoulder.
- **Shovel/digging stick**: hardwood, sharpened and fire-hardened (char lightly, scrape off).
- **Torch**: this device's light (torch button), or birch bark in a split stick.

Keep everything tied to you or in one place: losing a knife or lighter in the dark is a real disaster.
