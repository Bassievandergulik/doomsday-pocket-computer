# Burns

## Immediately
1. Stop the burning: smother flames (stop, drop, roll), move away from the source, switch off electricity first for electric burns.
2. **Cool with cool running water for 20 minutes.** Lukewarm or cool, not ice cold. Still useful up to 3 hours after the burn. Short on water? Use a basin and keep it cool; any clean cool water is better than none.
3. Remove rings, watches and clothing near the burn unless stuck to the skin.
4. Keep the rest of the person **warm**. Cooling large burns can cause hypothermia, especially in children.
5. Cover loosely with **cling film** (lay it on, don't wrap tightly) or a clean plastic bag on a hand. A clean non-fluffy cloth if nothing else.

## Do NOT
- Use butter, oil, toothpaste, flour or creams.
- Burst blisters.
- Use ice.

## Needs medical help when
- Bigger than the person's hand (palm + fingers ≈ 1% of body).
- Face, hands, feet, groin, joints, or goes all the way around a limb.
- White, leathery or charred skin, or painless burns (deep).
- Chemical or electrical burns, or smoke inhaled (hoarse voice, soot around the nose, cough).
- Children, elderly, or the person feels unwell.

## Chemical burns
Brush off powder first, then rinse with lots of water for at least 20 minutes. Eye: rinse with the injured eye lower, from the inside corner outwards, for 20 minutes.

## Sunburn
Cool showers, drink extra water, after-sun or aloe. Prevent with shade, clothing and hats. Blistered sunburn over large areas: treat as heat illness ([Heat](page:firstaid/heat)).

## Aftercare without a doctor
Keep the wound covered and clean, change dressings every 1–2 days, watch for infection: [Wounds](page:firstaid/wounds). Give extra fluids and paracetamol/ibuprofen for pain ([Medicines](page:firstaid/medicines)).
