# Severe bleeding

> Blood spurting, pooling or soaking through clothing can kill in minutes. Act before anything else except your own safety.

## Steps
1. **Press hard** directly on the wound with a pad, cloth or your hand (glove or plastic bag if possible). Keep pressing, do not peek, for at least **10 minutes**.
2. Blood comes through? Put more cloth on top and press harder. Do not remove the first pad.
3. **Pack deep wounds** (groin, armpit, neck, big gashes): stuff cloth/gauze firmly into the wound right down to where it bleeds, then press on top for 3–10 minutes.
4. Lay the person down, raise the legs if no leg injury (helps against shock: [Shock](page:firstaid/shock)).
5. Bandage firmly over the pad. Check fingers/toes beyond it stay pink and warm.

## Tourniquet (arms and legs only)
Use when pressure fails or you cannot keep pressing (several casualties, need to move).
1. 5–7 cm **above** the wound, not on a joint. Use a commercial tourniquet, or a 4 cm wide strip of cloth (never thin cord or wire).
2. Tie around the limb, put a stick in the knot and **twist until the bleeding stops**. It will hurt. That is normal.
3. Lock the stick in place. Write the **time** on the person's forehead or the tourniquet.
4. Do not loosen it. It can safely stay for about 2 hours; doctors remove it.
5. Bleeding continues? Add a second tourniquet just above the first.

## Nosebleed
Sit leaning **forward**, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes, breathe through the mouth. Don't tip the head back.

## Internal bleeding signs
Pale, cold, sweaty, fast weak pulse, thirst, confusion after a blow or fall, swollen hard belly, coughing or vomiting blood. Treat for shock and get to medical help urgently. Nothing to eat or drink.
