# Emergency numbers & quick signals

## Phone numbers (Europe)
| Number | Use |
|---|---|
| **112** | All emergencies, all EU countries plus UK, CH, NO, IS, TR. Free, also from locked phones. |
| 116 000 | Missing children (EU) |
| 116 117 | Non-urgent medical advice (DE, AT, some others) |
| 0900-8844 | Police non-emergency (NL) |
| SMS 112 | Possible in many EU countries for deaf/hard of hearing; in some only after registering |

Tips: a phone with no signal from *your* network will still try other networks for 112. Text messages often get through when calls fail. Turn the phone off between tries to save battery.

## Distress signals anyone will recognise
- **Three of anything**: 3 fires in a triangle, 3 whistle blasts, 3 flashes, repeated.
- **Alpine distress signal**: 6 blasts/flashes per minute, 1 minute pause, repeat. Answer: 3 per minute.
- **SOS**: · · · − − − · · ·  (short short short, long long long, short short short).
- **Arms raised in a Y** = "Yes, I need help". One arm up, one down = "No help needed".
- **Ground signals** (make them 3+ m tall, contrasting colours): **V** = need help, **X** = need medical help, **→** = travelling this way, **N** = no, **Y** = yes.

More: [Signalling for rescue](page:comms/signals), [Radio guide](page:comms/radio).

## Radio emergency channels
| Radio | Channel |
|---|---|
| PMR446 walkie-talkies | Channel 8 (446.09375 MHz) is the informal emergency/call channel in much of Europe. Channel 1 is the most used. |
| CB radio (27 MHz) | Channel 9 emergency, channel 19 road traffic |
| Marine VHF | Channel 16 (156.8 MHz) distress and calling |
| Aviation | 121.5 MHz emergency (listen only unless licensed and in true distress) |
| Ham radio | 145.500 MHz (2 m calling), 433.500 MHz (70 cm calling); IARU emergency centre-of-activity 3.760, 7.110, 14.300 MHz |

The built-in receiver covers FM, MW, LW and shortwave, including USB/LSB, so you can listen to the ham emergency frequencies above. It cannot transmit.
