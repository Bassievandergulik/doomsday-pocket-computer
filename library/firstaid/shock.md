# Shock (circulation failure)

Shock means not enough blood reaches the organs, after blood loss, burns, severe vomiting/diarrhoea, infection or allergy. It is life-threatening.

## Signs
- Pale, grey or blue-tinged, cold clammy skin, sweating
- Fast, weak pulse; fast shallow breathing
- Thirst, nausea, dizziness, anxiety, then confusion, drowsiness

## Treatment
1. Treat the cause: stop bleeding, cool burns.
2. **Lay them down.** Raise the legs about 30 cm if no leg/hip/spine injury.
3. Loosen tight clothing. Keep them **warm**: blanket under and over them.
4. Reassure them. Don't leave them alone.
5. **Nothing to eat or drink** if medical help is coming soon (small sips of water only if help is many hours away and they are alert).
6. Check breathing and pulse every few minutes. Unconscious and breathing: [recovery position](page:firstaid/cpr).

## Anaphylaxis (severe allergic reaction)
Swelling of lips/tongue/throat, wheezing, hives, collapse within minutes of a sting, food or drug.
1. Use their **adrenaline auto-injector** (EpiPen, Jext, Emerade) into the outer thigh, through clothing, hold 10 seconds.
2. Lie them down with legs up. If breathing is hard let them sit up.
3. No improvement after 5 minutes: give a second injector if available.
4. Call 112. CPR if they stop breathing normally.
Antihistamine tablets help with itching and hives but **do not** treat anaphylaxis.
