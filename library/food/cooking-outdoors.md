# Cooking outdoors without a kitchen

Cooking kills germs and parasites in wild food and makes most plants easier to digest. Use a **small fire with lots of hot embers**: flames burn food outside and leave it raw inside.

## Boiling (best: nothing is lost, the broth has all the fat and goodness)
- Pot, tin can or mug on two stones or a green stick over the embers.
- No pot: hang a plastic bottle or bag full of water near (not in) the flames, or drop **hot stones** into water in a wooden bowl, bark box or a hole lined with plastic: see [Tools and cordage](page:wild/tools-and-cordage).

## Roasting on a stick
Thread fish or meat on a green (living) stick, push the stick into the ground at an angle over the embers, turn it now and then. A whole fish: through the mouth along the backbone.

## Plank or flat stone
Fillet the fish, peg it skin-side down on a split log leaning near the fire. Or heat a flat dry stone (not from a river) in the fire and cook on top like a pan.

## In the embers
- Wrap fish, roots or meat in foil, or in thick layers of wet green leaves (no poisonous plants) or clay, and bury in the embers: fish 10–20 minutes, roots and potatoes 30–60 minutes.
- Unpeeled roots, chestnuts (cut a cross in the shell first, or they burst) and eggs (prick a hole) straight in the warm ash.

## Earth oven (for large amounts)
Dig a hole 50 cm deep, line with dry stones, build a big fire on top for an hour, rake out the embers, put the wrapped food on the hot stones, cover with leaves, then soil. Leave for 2–4 hours.

## How to know it's done
- Meat and game: **no pink** left, juices clear, hot right through. Boar, bear and other wild pigs and carnivores: cook very well (trichinella worms).
- Fish: flesh is white and flakes off the bones easily.
- Birds: cook thoroughly; eggs until firm.

## Bread without an oven (bannock)
Flour, a pinch of salt, a little fat if you have it, water until a firm dough. Flatten and bake on a hot stone or in a pan, or wind a thin snake of dough around a stick and turn it over the embers until it sounds hollow.
