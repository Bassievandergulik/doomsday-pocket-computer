# Ship signal flags (International Code)

Ships and yachts everywhere use the same 26 letter flags. One flag alone has a fixed meaning; the most urgent ones are **O** (man overboard), **V** (need help), **W** (need a doctor) and the pair **N over C**.

> **Distress at sea:** flags **N above C**, a square flag with a ball above or below it, or a flag flown upside down all mean *I am in serious danger and need immediate help*. Use them together with flares, VHF channel 16 or a beacon if you have them.

## Useful two-letter signals
- **NC**: I am in distress and require immediate assistance.
- **AC**: I am abandoning my vessel.
- **MAA**: I request urgent medical advice.
- **CB**: I require immediate assistance.

## Reading them
Flags are read from the top down. Pennants with numbers exist too; on small boats you'll mostly see **A** (divers), **B** (fuel barges) and **Q** (arriving from abroad). A yellow **Q** flag on a boat in harbour means it hasn't been cleared by customs or health yet.
