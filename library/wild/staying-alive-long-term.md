# Making it last: camp, routine and morale

After the first days the danger changes: exhaustion, small injuries that get infected, bad water, and losing hope. A simple daily routine prevents most of it.

## Choosing a camp
- Close to water but **at least 50 m** from it and above flood level (look for debris lines on trees).
- Not under dead trees or branches ("widow makers"), not in hollows (cold air and water collect), not on game trails or dry riverbeds.
- Sheltered from wind, with firewood nearby, visible from the air for rescuers (or hidden, if you need to stay unseen).
- Toilet at least 60 m from water and camp, downhill and downwind; dig 15–20 cm deep and cover.

## Daily routine
1. **Morning**: check yourself and others for injuries, ticks, blisters, cold feet. Mark the day (notches, or the device's notes).
2. **Water** for the whole day, purified: [Purifying water](page:water/purifying).
3. **Firewood** for the night plus a spare pile kept dry.
4. **Food** gathering, fishing, checking lines.
5. **Signal** set out and maintained: [Signalling](page:comms/signals).
6. **Improve the shelter** a little every day.
7. **Evening**: dry clothes, charge the device by solar if possible, plan tomorrow.

## Health
- Wash hands before eating and after the toilet: diarrhoea from dirty hands is a leading killer in camps. [Hygiene](page:skills/hygiene).
- Clean every scratch the same day; check for redness and warmth. [Wounds](page:firstaid/wounds).
- Feet: dry socks at night, air your feet, treat hot spots before they blister.
- Sleep: 7–8 hours if at all possible; tired people make deadly mistakes.
- Teeth: chew a twig into a brush (willow, birch).

## Saving the battery
Turn the screen brightness down, use the torch sparingly, switch WiFi off, and shut the device down when not needed. Copy important information (map sketch, route, water spots) onto paper too.

## Morale
- People who survive usually have **a reason** (family, a promise) and **a plan**. Write both down.
- Break big problems into small tasks for today and celebrate each one done.
- Keep a routine, keep clean, keep busy; talk to others or to yourself out loud.
- In a group: share work fairly, decide together, rotate unpleasant jobs, eat together.
- Fear and sadness are normal. Accept them, then do the next small task.

## When to leave
Leave when rescue clearly isn't coming, water or food has run out, or the place is no longer safe. Go in daylight, rested and with water, along a stream (downstream leads to people), a coast or a road. Leave a clear note: who, when, which direction.
