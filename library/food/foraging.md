# Safe foraging in Europe

> **Only eat a plant if you are 100% sure what it is.** Several deadly European plants look like edible ones. Never eat wild mushrooms unless an expert has checked them in person: Death cap (*Amanita phalloides*) causes liver failure days later and is responsible for most mushroom deaths. Wild food adds variety and vitamins; it rarely provides enough calories to live on.

## Easy, hard-to-confuse plants
| Plant | Where/when | Eat | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stinging nettle** | Everywhere, spring best | Young tops, cooked like spinach or tea | Very nutritious. Cooking/drying removes the sting. Gloves. |
| **Dandelion** | Everywhere, all year | Young leaves raw, flowers, roots roasted | Bitter older leaves: boil and change water |
| **Blackberry/bramble** | Hedges, Aug–Oct | Berries | Also young leaves for tea |
| **Raspberry, bilberry (blauwe bosbes)** | Woods, heath, Jul–Sep | Berries | Bilberry: dark blue juice inside, grows low |
| **Hazelnut** | Hedges, woods, Sep–Oct | Nuts | High in fat and calories |
| **Sweet chestnut** (*Castanea*) | Woods, Oct | Roasted/boiled nuts | Spiky husk with many fine needles. **Horse chestnut** (conker, smooth round nut in a husk with few thick spikes) is **poisonous**. |
| **Beech nuts** | Beech woods, autumn | Small triangular nuts | Roast; don't eat large amounts raw |
| **Acorns** | Oak, autumn | Flour after leaching | Raw acorns are bitter and contain tannins: crush and soak in changing water for days until not bitter |
| **Wild garlic (daslook)** | Damp woods, Mar–May | Leaves | Must smell strongly of garlic when crushed. Look-alikes **Lily of the valley** and **Autumn crocus** are deadly and have no garlic smell. Smell every leaf separately (your hands keep the smell). |
| **Rose hips** | Hedges, Sep–Nov | Flesh (remove hairy seeds) | Very high vitamin C; tea |
| **Clover, chickweed** | Lawns, fields | Leaves, flowers | Small amounts |
| **Cattail / bulrush (lisdodde)** | Pond edges | Roots (starch), young shoots | Collect only from clean water |
| **Pine/spruce needles** | Conifers | Tea (vitamin C) | Avoid **yew** (flat soft needles, red berries): deadly. |

## Deadly plants to know (Europe)
- **Hemlock & hemlock water-dropwort** (white umbrella flowers, purple-blotched stems): avoid **all** carrot-family plants with umbrella flowers.
- **Foxglove**, **monkshood**, **deadly nightshade** (shiny black berries), **yew**, **lily of the valley**, **autumn crocus**, **cuckoo pint / lords-and-ladies** (red berry spike).
- Unknown white or red berries: assume poisonous.
- **Mushrooms**: don't eat wild mushrooms unless an experienced person identified that exact species. The **death cap** and **destroying angel** (white gills, ring on the stem, cup at the base) kill with liver failure after a quiet day or two, and cooking doesn't help. They give few calories anyway.

## Animal food
- Fish: line and hook, worms or insects as bait. Check fishing rules while they still apply.
- Insects: grasshoppers, crickets, mealworms are edible **cooked** (removes parasites). Avoid brightly coloured, hairy or smelly ones.
- Snails: purge 3 days (feed on clean leaves/flour), then cook thoroughly.
- Freshwater mussels: avoid (pollution and protected).
- Trapping and hunting are illegal for most people in normal times and need skill; consider them only in a genuine long-term survival situation.

## The "universal edibility test"
A last-resort way to try a plant you cannot identify, when you have no other food and expect to be stuck for a long time. It takes about **a day per plant part** and **does not protect** against the worst poisons, which act after many hours or days (death cap, hemlock water-dropwort, autumn crocus, yew). So: **never** test mushrooms, plants with umbrella-shaped flower heads, plants with milky sap, beans and seeds in pods, or anything on the [Deadly plants](page:plants/poisonous-plants) page. Pick a plant that grows in large amounts, so it's worth the effort.

1. **Fast for 8 hours** beforehand (water only) and eat nothing else during the test.
2. Test **one part** of **one plant** at a time: leaf, stem, root, flower or fruit each count separately.
3. **Smell** it crushed. Strong bitter or almond (peach-stone) smell: reject.
4. **Skin**: rub it on the inside of your wrist or elbow for 15 minutes, then wait 8 hours. Burning, itching, rash or swelling: reject.
5. **Cook** it if possible (boiling destroys some toxins); test it prepared exactly the way you will eat it.
6. **Lip**: touch a small piece to your lip for 3 minutes. Burning or tingling: reject.
7. **Tongue**: hold a piece on your tongue for 15 minutes without swallowing. Burning, numbness, very bitter or soapy taste: spit out and rinse.
8. **Chew** a pinch thoroughly and hold it in your mouth for 15 minutes **without swallowing**. Any bad reaction: spit out and rinse.
9. **Swallow** that pinch, then wait **8 hours** with nothing else to eat. Nausea, cramps, dizziness, numbness, diarrhoea: make yourself vomit, drink lots of water and don't eat that plant again.
10. No problems: eat about a **quarter cup** of the same part prepared the same way, wait another 8 hours. Still fine: that part, prepared that way, is probably safe in moderate amounts. Don't assume other parts of the plant are safe.
