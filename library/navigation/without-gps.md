# Navigating without GPS

Keep this device's battery for when you really need it. Learn to find direction without it.

## Map and compass basics
- **Compass needle** points to magnetic north. In the Netherlands/Belgium/Germany magnetic north is currently a few degrees **east** of true north (about +2° to +5°), small enough to ignore on foot for short distances. Set `magnetic_declination` in the config for the digital compass.
- **Take a bearing**: point the compass direction-of-travel arrow at the target, turn the housing until N matches the needle, read the degrees. To follow: keep the needle in the "shed", walk where the arrow points.

![Taking a bearing](fig:compass-bearing.svg)

- **Back bearing** (to return): add or subtract 180°.
- Pick a landmark on your bearing, walk to it, repeat. Better than staring at the compass.
- **Aiming off**: aim deliberately to one side of a target on a line feature (road, river), then you know which way to turn when you reach it.

## Distance by pace counting
Count every second step (each time the left foot lands). Typical adult: **60–65 double paces per 100 m** on flat ground; more uphill, in woods or at night. Measure your own on a known 100 m stretch. Use a knot in a cord every 100 m.

## Sun
- The sun rises roughly east and sets roughly west (in Europe: north-east and north-west in summer, south-east and south-west in winter).
- **At solar noon the sun is due south** (northern hemisphere). In Central European Time solar noon is about **12:30–13:00 in winter, 13:30–14:00 in summer** in NL/BE (later further west).
- **Watch method** (analog watch, northern hemisphere): point the hour hand at the sun. South is halfway between the hour hand and 12 (use 1 o'clock during summer time).

![Watch method (example: 4 o'clock)](fig:watch-method.svg)

- **Shadow stick**: stick upright, mark the shadow tip, wait 15–20 minutes, mark again. A line from the first to the second mark points roughly **east**.

![Shadow stick](fig:shadow-stick.svg)

## Stars
- **Polaris** (North Star) is within 1° of true north. Find the **Big Dipper / Plough** (Grote Beer): extend the line through the two end stars of the "pan" about 5 times their distance. Polaris is the end of the Little Dipper's handle, not very bright.
- **Cassiopeia** (W shape) is on the opposite side of Polaris from the Plough.

![Finding the North Star](fig:stars-north.svg)

- **Orion** rises due east and sets due west; his sword points roughly south.
- Southern hemisphere: Southern Cross, extend the long axis 4.5× to the south celestial pole.

![Southern Cross](fig:southern-cross.svg)

## Moon
A crescent moon: draw a line through the two horns down to the horizon: that is roughly south (northern hemisphere).

![Crescent moon](fig:moon-south.svg)

## Nature signs (unreliable alone)
Moss and lichen grow on the damp side of trees (often north in Europe, but local shade dominates); trees bend away from the prevailing wind (south-west in NW Europe); snow lasts longer on north-facing slopes; churches are often built with the altar end facing east.

## Lost?
STOP. Go back to the last place you were sure of. Follow a stream downhill towards habitation (in Europe you will almost always reach a road within a day). Mark your route.
