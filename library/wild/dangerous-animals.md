# Bites, stings and dangerous animals

Most animals avoid people. Give them space, make noise when walking in thick cover, never corner or feed them, and keep food away from where you sleep.

## Ticks (the most common real danger in NW Europe)
- Check your whole body every evening, especially knees, groin, armpits, hairline. Children: head and neck.
- Remove **as soon as possible** with tweezers or a tick card: grab close to the skin, pull straight out slowly. No oil, alcohol or burning.
- Mark the date. A **red ring growing** around the bite after 3–30 days, or flu-like fever, means Lyme disease: needs antibiotics.

## Snakes
- Europe: the **adder** (zigzag on the back) is the only venomous snake in NL, rare in the UK/Germany too. Bites are seldom deadly but need a hospital.
- Any venomous bite: **keep calm and still**, remove rings and watches, splint the limb at heart level, walk out only if you must. No cutting, sucking, ice or tourniquet. Note the time and what the snake looked like.

## Insects
- **Wasps and bees**: scrape a bee sting out sideways. Swelling of the face, throat or trouble breathing = **anaphylaxis**: adrenaline pen if available, call for help, lie down with legs raised (sit up if breathing is hard).
- **Oak processionary caterpillars** (Netherlands, summer): their hairs cause itchy rash and eye problems. Don't touch nests; rinse skin, change clothes.
- **Mosquitoes**: in the tropics they carry malaria and dengue. Nets, long sleeves, repellent at dusk.

## In and near water
- **Weever fish** (Dutch: *pieterman*, North Sea beaches): stepping on its spines is very painful. Put the foot in water **as hot as you can stand** (about 45 °C) for 30–90 min: heat breaks the venom down.
- **Jellyfish**: get out, rinse with **seawater** (not fresh), remove tentacles with a card or tweezers, then hot water. Vinegar only for tropical box jellyfish.
- **Sea urchins, lionfish, fire coral**: see [Heat, desert and tropical islands](page:wild/hot-climates).
- **Blue-green algae** (paint-like scum on lakes in summer): don't swim or drink, even boiled.

## Mammals
- **Wild boar** (forests in NL, BE, DE), especially with young: back away slowly, climb or put a tree between you. Don't run straight away.
- **Dogs**: don't stare or run; stand sideways, arms in, offer a bag or jacket to bite. Any mammal bite: wash 15 minutes with soap and water; rabies is a risk outside Western Europe and from bats everywhere: needs vaccination quickly.
- **Bears** (Scandinavia, Eastern Europe, Pyrenees): make noise, store food 100 m from camp. If one approaches: talk calmly, back away; never run. If attacked by a brown bear: lie on your belly, hands on neck, play dead.
- **Wolves** almost never attack people. Stand tall, shout, throw things, back away.

## Scorpions and spiders
Shake out shoes, clothes and sleeping bags in warm countries. Most stings are painful but not deadly: cold compress, rest, painkillers. Children, elderly and anyone with breathing problems need help quickly.
